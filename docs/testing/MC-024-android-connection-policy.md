# Android connection and power integration

MC-024 connects the existing Rust power/handshake owners to the MC-023 Android driver. All data and measurements in automated tests are synthetic. Hardware, OEM, battery and production key-protection acceptance remain MC-025/027/043/044.

## Core boundary

`NativeTransport.update_power` accepts Auto/Normal/Saver, battery percentage, charging, locally observed peer count and suspend-inclusive monotonic time. A null setting retains the current selection and Auto hysteresis. The existing `power::Policy` determines thresholds, the 60-second hysteresis and scan/ANNOUNCE intervals. The bridge applies `Ingress::set_link_limit` and `Relay::set_power` without recreating their owners or refunding credits. Shrinking the cap cancels newest setup permits first, then newest ready links, returning every terminal effect. Android releases matching native resources. Beacon remains MC-033.

Both proof receipt and local proof completion invoke the existing `Friends::duplicate_links_to_close` decision. Only two independently proved sessions can be consolidated; a sole asymmetric connection and unproved HELLO claims cannot trigger that decision. The common initiator-key/nonce ordering is unchanged. There is no new wire contract or trust rule.

The bounded observation API distinguishes activity from content acceptance. Text-valid, budget-admitted unsigned CHAT/ANNOUNCE qualifies. Full-key friend-signed CHAT/ANNOUNCE qualifies only after a separate, charged strict Ed25519/key-hash/text check using the existing verifier and immutable friend-sign/v1 transcript. A committed Node/OpenSSL friend vector and a corrupted variant exercise this across the Rust and native bindings. The check does not resolve pending content, write history, pin identity or produce a trusted display event. A later content owner still pays its own acceptance work. This extra work is included in the existing ingress signature budget. Missing keys, exhausted work credit, unverified signatures, encrypted/opaque data and proof/HELLO traffic do not become qualifying CHAT/ANNOUNCE. Feature integration must send its initial valid public ANNOUNCE (full key when signed) within the connection window; encrypted message traffic alone is not proof of a peer's valid public announcement.

First and latest qualifying receipt times remain separate. Keeping the first time prevents an overdue callback from disguising a missed initial deadline. Up to 200 locally observed CHAT IDs expire after 60 seconds; peer 256-byte ANNOUNCE digests also expire after 60 seconds. The documented SHA-256 double-hash/MSB-first digest estimates the fraction of local IDs absent at the peer, in per-mille units. Empty/expired samples mean unknown, not zero overlap. These are advisory hints, never identity or delivery evidence.

## Android selection and bounds

`ConnectionPolicy` is the production policy used by `AndroidGattRadio`, with at most 64 discovery/retry/blacklist records and six total Normal connections including setup (three in Saver). Full record capacity drops new observations until an expired record can be reused; unexpired blacklist records are retained. Node/address admission still belongs to Rust, so address churn or a local retry cannot refill it.

Selection prefers unseen addresses, then fresh advisory novelty and stronger measured RSSI. Every 60 seconds the best locally observed links occupy the stable slots; ties preserve older links. Up to two exploration slots rotate when candidates exist (`min(2,L-1)` under a lower cap), retaining at least one stable slot. More novel peers can replace weaker stable choices at subsequent evaluations. Reconnect attempts wait at least five seconds; rotated peers wait sixty seconds. Inbound setup bypasses the outgoing retry timer so simultaneous central/peripheral attempts do not deadlock; it still obeys the blacklist, slot ceiling, RSSI bound and core admission budgets.

The RSSI clustering heuristic uses fixed ten-dB bins, at most three measured links per bin, with reevaluation after measurements change. This is an explicit implementation choice for the design's near-identical-RSSI heuristic, not physical distance or Sybil detection. Unknown RSSI is not invented. Location/proximity denial clears stored RSSI and disables this scoring input.

A peer with no qualifying first CHAT/ANNOUNCE within twenty seconds of native connection reservation is closed and its observed address blacklisted for five minutes. Repeated advertisements cannot reset that ban. Later ordinary thirty-second announcements do not trigger a contradictory twenty-second continuous-idle timeout. Native setup/HELLO/send deadlines remain independently enforced. Android's existing whole-server epoch replacement may close other peripheral links during a teardown; they reconnect through the same policy and budgets rather than retaining stale callbacks. This conservative behavior remains a physical acceptance concern.

## Scanning, permissions and lifecycle

Normal uses balanced scanning with ten-second low-latency bursts while isolated. Before five minutes, a burst alternates with ten seconds balanced; after five minutes the burst period is thirty seconds, and after fifteen minutes sixty seconds. Saver uses ten seconds on/fifty off. Discovery and actual app foregrounding reset isolation; a foreground service alone is not treated as foreground UI. Late ticks do not replay missed bursts. Scan failure retries use five/ten/twenty/forty/sixty-second bounded backoff, with a visible discovery-paused state.

The manifest keeps the location-disclosed path and does not assert `neverForLocation`. Fine-location permission and enabled location services gate scanning; on API29/30, background discovery additionally requires background location. Bluetooth permission/radio loss and device locking tear down the service generation. On API31+, missing location can leave existing connections/advertising available while discovery is explicitly limited and RSSI is disabled. The policy never claims an active scan without the applicable permissions. It does not request GPS samples or transmit location.

The service accepts a status callback and updates its notification for limited discovery. `currentState` reports stopped, permission/radio/lock failure, location restrictions or scan backoff; it does not promise survival after process death. `START_NOT_STICKY` retains no provider/core session across a process restart. The application must reopen its protected owner through a visible authorized start. Controlled tests inject stop and stale callbacks; actual OEM termination remains MC-025. `powerSetting` changes the user choice and `currentPower` exposes the applied tier, link ceiling and ANNOUNCE interval to the later application announcement/UI owner. This ticket does not create unsigned placeholder announcements or retain unlocked private keys.
